# Orbit design system

Orbit looks like one product across the browser frame, Settings and the other built-in pages, the
new tab page and Shields. Colors come from one set of tokens in `tools/design/tokens.mjs`.

## Idea

A quiet instrument around one striking view. The browser frame and pages are calm graphite or
white with a single periwinkle accent, so content and the person's own work lead. Space appears in
one place, on purpose: the new tab page opens on NASA's black hole visualization, with the clock
set inside the black hole's shadow. Everything over that picture is dark glass, so text stays
readable on any frame of the video or any image the person chooses.

The mark is a white open ring with a satellite on a black rounded square. The same ring and
satellite appear around a shortcut on the new tab page when it is hovered or focused.

## Themes

| Theme | What it is | How it is built |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dark | Neutral graphite, periwinkle accent | Orbit's palette in dark mode |
| Light | White surfaces, lavender frame | Orbit's palette in light mode |
| System | Dark or Light, following the device | Chromium's device mode |

People choose Light, Dark or Device in Settings > Appearance > Mode; Device is the default, so
Orbit respects the system and accessibility setting. Orbit's own pages follow the
browser through `prefers-color-scheme`. The new tab page's background is chosen separately, in its
Customize panel: Orbit's black hole video, the person's own image or video, or a plain surface.

## Tokens

`tools/design/tokens.mjs` builds Orbit's tonal ramps (primary, secondary, tertiary, neutral,
neutral variant; the error ramp is Chromium's) for dark and light mode and writes:

- `chromium/overlay/ui/color/orbit_palette.inc`: Chromium's reference colors. `orbit-theme.patch`
  uses them in place of Chromium's default palette, so the frame, tabs, toolbar, address bar,
  menus, dialogs, side panel and WebUI pages all take them.
- `extension/src/shared/tokens.css`: the same values as CSS variables for the built-in pages, plus
  the media accent (`--orbit-media-primary`), which stays the dark-mode accent in both themes for
  controls drawn over photos and video.
- The contrast table below.

Error pages and security warnings get fixed values from the same palette in `orbit-pages.patch`.
A page that could not load shows Orbit's open ring with its satellite drifted off the path, the
ring muted and the satellite in the accent color (`orbit-error-page.patch`).

Over photos and video the new tab page does not rely on these pairs alone. Text sits on dark glass
(near-black at 56 to 60 percent behind a blur), the backdrop has a vignette that darkens the edges,
top and bottom where controls sit, and focus rings drawn over the picture are white with a dark
outer edge, so they show on bright and dark parts of a frame alike.

## Type

Built-in pages use Readex Pro (SIL Open Font License), which covers Latin and Arabic, so both
scripts share one voice. Weights: 190 for the clock, 200 to 300 for large numbers, 320 to 350 for
reading text, 450 to 500 for labels and headings. Sizes step from 12 px (credits) through 13, 15
and 17 px to the clock, which scales with the window between 52 and 140 px. Labels are sentence
case. Native browser UI keeps the system font.

## New tab page

```
 shortcuts (scrolls sideways when they do not fit)                  [customize]

                               11:15 PM
                          Thursday, November 12
                  [ search or enter an address        ]

   [ Protection            ]  [ Weather    ]  [ Notes                ]
 [pause] Video: NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center/Jeremy Schnittman
```

- The clock and search sit at the exact center of the window, where the black hole's shadow is,
  until the widgets need more room on short windows.
- Radii follow hierarchy: cards 24 px, items inside cards 14 px, controls and the search field
  fully rounded, shortcut icons circular.
- Widgets are Protection (local block counts with the reporting period), Weather and Notes. Each
  can be hidden, resized (small, medium, large) and reordered in Customize, or dragged while
  Customize is open.
- The background video pauses when the tab is hidden, when the person presses pause (remembered),
  and never plays when the device asks for reduced motion; the first frame shows instead.
- A person's own image or video is stored in IndexedDB on the device, up to 200 MB.

## Motion

Motion answers what people do: the Customize panel slides in, the shortcut ring fades in on hover
and focus, meters fill when counts change. The only motion nobody asked for is the background
video, and it has a pause control. All transitions stop under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Window

- One top row instead of Chromium's tab strip above a toolbar: back, forward and reload, a
  480 px address bar, then the tabs, then extensions and the menu, beside the window controls
  (`orbit-window.patch`). The toolbar keeps an empty place after the address bar and the browser
  layout puts the tab strip over it. Empty space in the row moves the window.
- The horizon: a 1 px line under the row that runs from faint periwinkle through amber and rose
  (the black hole's disk) to periwinkle. It replaces Chromium's grey separator.
- The row takes the frame color; the active tab keeps the lighter base color, so it reads as a
  raised pill.
- Tabs are pills. Every tab, the active one included, is a detached shape that floats in the tab
  strip with 6 px above and below it, instead of Chromium's active tab that flares into the
  toolbar. The active tab takes the toolbar color; others show a fill only on hover. There are no
  separator lines between tabs (`orbit-tabs.patch`).
- Toolbar and tab icons are Orbit's own: thin round-capped lines on Chromium's 20 px grid
  (back and forward chevrons, reload as an open ring with its gap at the top right like the mark,
  a house, three dots for the menu, a puzzle piece with a slash or a check for its blocked and
  allowed states, download, close and new tab). They are drawn once in
  `tools/design/browser-icons.mjs`, which writes Chromium's `.icon` files into the overlay in
  place of the ones Chromium 157's toolbar uses, together with every copy of Orbit's mark.
- Chromium's test field trials are off (`disable_fieldtrial_testing_config` in
  `tools/chromium/args.gn`). They turned on lab experiments, among them toolbar buttons drawn as
  web pages with Chromium's icons.

## Address bar

Internal pages show as `orbit://` in the address bar, with Orbit's mark as their icon. Pages of
Orbit's built-in extension show as `orbit://<page>`, such as `orbit://privacy`, instead of a
`chrome-extension://` address. Typing an `orbit://` address opens the matching page
(`orbit-scheme.patch`). Only the address bar changes: web pages still cannot open internal pages,
with either scheme.

## Right to left

Built-in pages set `dir` from the browser language through `chrome.i18n` and use logical CSS
properties (`inset-inline`, `margin-inline`, `padding-inline`), so Arabic mirrors the layout. Web
addresses, host names and the logo stay left to right. Text people type (notes) uses `dir="auto"`.

## Limits

- Address bar suggestions for built-in pages still show `chrome://` while typing.
- Windows and Linux frames are opaque. The translucent frame option in Chromium 157 is macOS only.
- The address bar keeps Chromium's rounded shape and layout, and icons outside the toolbar and
  tab strip (menus, page info, side panel) are still Chromium's.
